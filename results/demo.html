HashAccount: an Ethereum account with no ECDSA key

Hash-based one-time signatures (Winternitz, in a Merkle tree of 1,024 keys), verified in an EIP-8141 frame transaction. Each key is spent through the transaction nonce. Hash-based, believed to resist known quantum attacks. Unaudited research code.

Results marked LOCAL come from our own ethrex eip8141-tracer node, sidecar and block driver, not the public devnet. Public-devnet results: pending. Generated by scripts/demo_page.py from results/.

Key rotation (LOCAL)

When a tree of 1,024 keys runs low, one transaction signed by the current tree registers the next one. On the local chain the rotation was included in block 2 (status 0x1), the account stored the new tree's commitment (matches), and a payment signed with the new tree was included in block 3 (status 0x1, VERIFY 90,240 gas). A key tree that was never registered is refused.

Heavy signatures and the alternative mempool

Profile B (XMSS) needs far more than 100,000 gas. The sidecar we ran refuses it: validation prefix execution gas sum 900000 + signature-verification cost 100 = 900100 exceeds MAX_VERIFY_GAS 100000. With our proposed fix (a configurable budget and the standard precompiles allowed), the sidecar accepted it, relayed it, and it was included in block 2 with status 0x1. See docs/RULE_FINDINGS.md.

Deploying the account inside the same transaction needs about 2,740,417 state gas; the public cap is 500,000, so the account is deployed first.

Why reuse matters

With toy 16-bit digits, a stand-in attacker forged 200 of 200 keys that signed twice (median 17 attempts), against 58 of 200 keys that signed once (median 10,230 attempts). Full view: results/attack.html.
